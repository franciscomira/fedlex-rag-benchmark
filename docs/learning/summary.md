# What I learned from the Phase 3 fine-tune

Written for Francisco, in English only, tied to this project's own numbers. The questions at the end are the ones an interviewer is most likely to ask about this work; the answers are drafts to rewrite in your own words.

## The concepts, in the order we met them

1. **Bi-encoder against cross-encoder.** A bi-encoder embeds the query and each article separately, so the 11,000 article vectors are computed once and a search is one dot product. A cross-encoder reads query and article together, which is more accurate but cannot be precomputed, so it only reorders a short list. Phase 2's finding that the *first stage* was the bottleneck (the right article was not among the candidates for 86% of English questions) is why we tuned the bi-encoder first.
2. **Contrastive training with in-batch negatives.** The loss does not teach the answer; it teaches which vectors should be close. For a batch of 64 (question, article) pairs, every other article in the batch is a wrong answer for this question. That is 63 free negatives per question, and it is the reason batch size matters and why two questions about the same article must never share a batch.
3. **Positives, negatives and false negatives.** The same article in German, French and Italian is one positive. A "negative" that also answers the question (a false negative) pushes a correct answer away. Audited on 100 sampled questions with a judge from another model family: 1 of 100 filtered hard negatives also answered the question, 4 of 100 without the filter.
4. **Leakage.** Dev and test gold articles, their neighbours, and near-copies were excluded from the training pool by code, with tests that fail on any overlap. A deliberately poisoned pool made the tests fail. This is what lets the dev and test numbers mean anything.
5. **The knobs.** Learning rate 2e-5 with 10% warmup and linear decay, batch 64, 3 epochs. Loss fell from 3.1 to 0.3 in the first 180 steps; epochs 2 and 3 added about 1 point of validation recall, inside the noise.
6. **Overfitting is read from two curves.** Training loss always falls; validation loss on articles never seen is the honest one. Here validation loss kept falling and ended close to training loss, so there was no overfitting at 11.5k pairs.
7. **Ablations.** Hard negatives made no measurable difference on dev (+0.017 recall@10, interval touching zero) and cost 1.7 times the training time. A quarter of the data (2,876 questions) already gave most of the dev gain (recall@10 0.53 to 0.76 against 0.79 with all of it).
8. **Intervals decide what you may claim.** Dev has about 40 question groups, so intervals are 30 points wide: validation (1,296 questions) was used for decisions between runs, dev only to confirm. On test, T2 against the LLM-translation pipeline differs by +0.015 recall@10 with an interval from -0.05 to +0.07: not claimable either way.
9. **Check your instruments, not just your model.** Two numbers for the same untrained model (0.78 on validation, 0.44 in my mining) did not agree; the cause was a bug in the Phase 2 embedder (padding tokens averaged into batched vectors), found by comparing torch with ONNX outputs. It changed the corrected baselines (system A on test: 0.49 to 0.55), invalidated my hard-negative mining and the "hub articles" finding, and was written up as an erratum. A surprising result from a tool you trust is a reason to test the tool.

## Five questions an interviewer is likely to ask (drafts)

1. **Why fine-tune an embedding model rather than a reranker or just use an LLM?** Phase 2 showed that once the right article is in the candidate list, small and large LLM rerankers all rank it first; the loss was before that, in first-stage retrieval, especially English questions against German, French and Italian text (14% recall@10, later corrected to 24%). A bi-encoder is also the only stage that can be precomputed over the whole corpus, so improving it costs nothing at query time.
2. **How did you make sure the model was not trained on the test questions?** The training questions were synthetic, written by an LLM from articles outside the pool; the pool excluded every dev and test gold article, its neighbours (previous and next article) and any article with at least 50% character 5-gram overlap with a gold article, and tests fail on any overlap in the pool, the positives and the negatives. The benchmark's questions were never shown to the generator. What is not excluded: the same drafting model family wrote training and benchmark questions, so style leakage is limited by varying style, not removed; and a review found that short pool articles contained in a long gold article (37 at 60% containment) slipped past the symmetric overlap rule, which I disclosed and checked (the test gain is unchanged without the affected items) instead of retraining.
3. **How do you know the improvement is real and not noise?** Paired bootstrap over question groups (a question and its translations resample together), reported with every number. T1 against the corrected untuned hybrid: +0.26 recall@10 [+0.17, +0.35] on 164 test items (it changes the weights and drops BM25 at once; on dev the untuned model scored the same either way). I also say what is not claimable: T1 against the LLM-translation pipeline on recall@10 (-0.06 [-0.15, +0.02]), and the hard-negative result.
4. **What went wrong?** A padding bug in my own Phase 2 embedder degraded every embedding-based retrieval number and, through it, my first hard-negative mining. I found it because validation (0.78) and mining (0.44) disagreed for the same model, traced it to a mask over padding, fixed it with a regression test, re-measured system A on test (0.49 to 0.55), reran B and C retrieval-only, and wrote an erratum. The hard negatives that I had built and audited on the buggy vectors were rebuilt and then turned out not to help.
5. **Would you ship this?** For a first stage, yes: 0.81 recall@10 on test at 0 cost and 0.1 s, against 0.87 for an LLM-translation pipeline at 0.0024 USD and 8 s. With a cheap reranker on top (T2) it matches that pipeline (0.89 against 0.87, not distinguishable) at a fifth of the latency and two thirds of the cost. Not yet claimable: that answers are better, because no answers were generated or judged in Phase 3, and the benchmark rewards finding the article an LLM drafted the question from, so I would test on real user questions and Swiss-law expert review first.
