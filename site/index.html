1. The answer in short

The question: can a small fine-tuned multilingual model match an LLM-based pipeline at finding the right article of Swiss federal law, for questions in German, French, Italian and English, at a fraction of the cost? What was tuned is the search embedder, not a reranker, because the first search step, not the reranker, limited the results of Phase 2. The only LLM in the compared pipelines is a small one (claude-haiku-5-5); no frontier-model reranker was compared on the test questions.

On 164 answerable test questions (retrieval only, 95% intervals):

  • T1, the fine-tuned embedder alone (no LLM call, 0.0000 [0.0000, 0.0000] USD per question, median 0.07 s): recall@10 0.81 [0.71, 0.90].
  • Against the untuned hybrid search (A corrected, 0.55 [0.46, 0.63]): T1 minus A is +0.262 [+0.174, +0.351], claimable, the interval excludes 0.
  • Against LLM translation (C': an LLM translates the question first; recall@10 0.87 [0.79, 0.94], 0.0024 [0.0024, 0.0025] USD per question, median 8.0 s): T1 minus C' is -0.064 [-0.149, +0.016] on recall@10 (not claimable, because the interval includes 0) and -0.207 [-0.305, -0.106] on MRR, the rank of the right article (claimable, the interval excludes 0).
  • With a cheap LLM reranker on top (T2: recall@10 0.89 [0.80, 0.97], 0.0016 [0.0016, 0.0017] USD per question, 32% cheaper than C', 5.0 times faster at the median): T2 minus C' is +0.015 [-0.052, +0.074] on recall@10 (not claimable, because the interval includes 0).

These are retrieval results only: they say how often the right article is found, not that final answers are better. Answer-level results for the tuned pipeline are pending (section 4). The questions were written by an LLM from the text of the article they ask about.

Erratum: the embedder padding bug

What was wrong

The program that turns text into vectors (the embedder) had a bug when it handled several texts at once. Shorter texts are padded to the length of the longest one in the batch, and the padding was wrongly averaged into the vector of each text. A question embedded alone, as at search time, was correct. Every article of the search index was embedded in batches, so the article vectors were slightly off, most for short texts that shared a batch with long ones. It was found in Phase 3, when the same untrained model scored much better in a second implementation than in the project's own pipeline. It is fixed, with a regression test, and the old behaviour is kept behind a switch so the old numbers can still be reproduced.

What was re-measured

Corrected system A is run 2026-10-28_test_75cfac7c94, frozen as A_padfix (config 75cfac7c94); it is retrieval only (no model call, no cost) and the second use of the test split for system A. B' and C' are B and C without the answer step, with the corrected embedder, also run once on test. The old and the new rows are different runs.

What is not known

B, C and D used the same weakened first search step, so their candidate lists were worse than the model allows. Their reranker, answerer and judge numbers are what they are, but their recall, fact recall and the gaps between the systems were measured on degraded candidates and might change in either direction. A better first stage helps the single-shot system B most, which narrows the gap that query translation closes. Fact recall, faithfulness and trap results were not re-measured, and the agent D was not rerun. Read the Phase 2 answer claims as conditional on the buggy first stage until the frozen B, C and D are rerun with the corrected embedder; that decision is still open.

5. Limitations

The questions and the data

  • The benchmark questions were written by an LLM from the text of one article, and the training questions by the same kind of model. The results show how well systems find the article a question was written from, not how people really ask. Part of the fine-tuning gain may be the model learning that writing style.
  • The test set is small: 164 answerable questions in 110 independent groups (a question and its translations count once), and 39 to 43 per language. Only large differences can be claimed.
  • Nobody on the project reads German, French and Italian well enough to have reviewed the training questions; only a sample was audited, by a second AI model. No Swiss-law expert reviewed the benchmark, its gold facts or any answer; the one human reviewer reads English only and has no legal training.
  • Some short training articles are partly contained in longer test gold articles, so a few test questions may be easier for the tuned model than for a fresh one. The Phase 3 report checks that the headline gain does not depend on them.

How answers are scored

  • One judge model (gpt-6.1-sol) scores all answers. It agreed with one human on 81% of the facts in a small sample, so differences of a few points in fact recall or faithfulness are noise that the intervals do not include.
  • Trap questions are few (15). They are reported as counts, not ranked.

What was measured

  • The corrected systems (A corrected, B', C', T1, T2) were measured on retrieval only. Nothing shows yet that the tuned embedder improves final answers; the Phase 4 rows are meant to test that.
  • System D, the agent, was not re-measured with the corrected embedder, and the Phase 2 answer-level results for B, C and D predate the fix.
  • After tuning, embedding alone beat the hybrid with keyword search on the development questions, but that was not tested further and was not run on the test split. T1 and T2 use embedding only.
  • Each configuration was run once, with one training seed; run-to-run variation is not measured. Model answers are not bit-reproducible, so numbers replay from a cache of recorded responses, not from scratch.
  • The development split was used to choose configurations; the test split was used once per frozen configuration, twice for system A, and B' was frozen after the T1, T2 and C' results were known.

Speed and cost

  • Latency comes from one Windows laptop on one day with serial calls; cached model calls are replayed, so it shows the relative cost of pipelines, not provider speed. Costs use the price table of 2026-10-17.

Provenance

  • The trained weights are not stored in git. The configuration of T1 records a digest of the weights, and the weights are published on Hugging Face. One training run's configuration does not record the code version.
  • A planned feature of the synthetic training questions (a fixed asker role per question) was never applied: the generator invented the roles.
